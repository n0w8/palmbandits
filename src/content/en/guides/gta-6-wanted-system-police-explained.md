The police in GTA 6 are no longer the all-knowing pursuers of GTA 5. They only know what witnesses, cameras and alarms tell them, and they search for a description. That makes escapes more tactical, but also nastier if you get careless. Here's the full system as the reviews describe it, and how to actually lose the cops.

**As of November 18, 2026, 5 p.m. CET.** This is based on the reviews that went live at noon CET today and on the August previews. The basics in short form are in our [tips for your first hours](/en/guides/gta-6-first-hours-tips-gameplay-systems/). This guide goes deeper.

## The quick version

- **Up to six stars**, depending on how serious the crime is.
- **The police only show up** if someone sees you, an alarm goes off, a camera records you or a stolen car's GPS tracker pings.
- **They search by traits:** appearance, clothing, vehicle, visible weapon, your partner, camera footage.
- **Heat lingers after an escape:** a zone around your last known location that slowly cools off.

## How a manhunt plays out

Press Start broke down the stages from the game. An icon under the stars always tells you where you stand:

| Stage | What's happening | What you should do |
|---|---|---|
| Cops Responding | a crime was reported, units are heading to the scene | leave now, it's the best moment to escape |
| Suspects Unknown | reported, but nobody knows it was you | keep a low profile |
| In Pursuit | the police know who you are and can see you | break line of sight |
| Visual Lost | search with helicopter spotlights and K9 units | hide, change your traits |
| Heat | you're out of the search area, but the zone stays hot | avoid the area |
| Heat Suspect Unknown | no description, but locals are on higher alert | don't commit another crime there |

The color of the stars tells you something too. According to the previews, solid white stars mean direct line of sight and flashing white stars mean they've lost you. Outlined stars stand for a reported crime without a description, and red ones for the search area after an escape.

## What the police know about you

According to GamesRadar+, the Heat system can identify you after a crime through these traits:

- your appearance and outfit
- your vehicle
- a visible weapon
- your partner's appearance
- security camera footage

Each one can be defused separately, and that's the heart of the system. PlayStation LifeStyle says you can even return to the scene in a new outfit without being recognized. TheGamer points out that officers radio in a sighting before they've positively identified you, which gives you a short window to react.

## What each star level brings

- **One star:** easy to shake, especially if nobody has identified you yet.
- **Mid levels:** reinforcements, roadblocks and thorough area searches, per PlayStation Universe. Shacknews says choppers get called in over the radio.
- **Five stars:** TrueAchievements was met by NOOSE units with machine-gun-mounted Humvees and attack helicopters.
- **Six stars:** TrueAchievements says the game makes it clear the police won't stop until you're dead. Previews say that at the highest level, they search the entire map.

For context: Press Start's writer only reached five stars in more than 60 hours. You'll have to work hard to provoke six.

## Losing the cops: the methods that work

1. **Leave early:** While the police are still on their way, getting away is easiest.
2. **Split up:** Vice says the police look for a duo. If Jason and Lucia go separate ways, the search gets harder.
3. **Switch cars:** A new car or a trip through a Pay 'n' Spray clears the heat on your vehicle. Just remember a new car might have a tracker of its own.
4. **Change your outfit:** Spare clothes from the trunk, a store or a salon. Previews say hats and glasses aren't enough, but a full outfit change or a new hairstyle is.
5. **Wear a mask:** With a mask on, there's no usable description. You can toss it during the getaway.
6. **Ditch your weapons:** A visible weapon is a trait the police look for.
7. **Destroy evidence:** Vice and Press Start say you can wipe camera footage, even by going back to the scene afterwards.
8. **Stay upwind:** Press Start says police dogs follow your scent even after everything else has been cleared.
9. **Duck:** PlayStation Universe says you can duck behind the wheel when patrol cars pass.

One gripe from Shacknews: in many missions, the GPS only shows your route once you've lost the police. Vice finds the police presence overwhelming at times, but stresses that the game gives you plenty of tools to cool things down.

## Criminal Profile: what your behavior triggers

The Criminal Profile tracks how Jason and Lucia carry themselves as criminals. According to Shacknews, the tiers run from best to worst:

1. **Professional:** clean getaways, few casualties
2. **Hardened**
3. **Violent**
4. **Psychopath**
5. **Irredeemable:** per Shacknews, Trevor territory with no way back

Shacknews says the game distinguishes between cold-blooded murder and shooting back. Its reviewer stayed "Professional" despite 457 kills. Kotaku aimed for kneecaps to avoid being labeled a psycho, and says the profile affects how characters react and how aggressively the police come after you. According to Vice, a good profile unlocks new crimes. And at the bottom end, it also strains the relationship between the two.

## Busted: what an arrest costs you

According to previews, getting "Busted" or "Wasted" costs you the cash you're carrying. Money in the bank stays put. And when Jason and Lucia are out together, one can get arrested while the other escapes.

## Our tip

Think like a pro, not a rampager. Before a job, check for cameras, put on a mask, keep spare clothes in the trunk and pick a getaway car without a tracker. Afterwards, split up, change clothes, swap cars and only then head back to the safehouse. Play like that and, per PlayStation Universe, the police will mostly leave you alone. Your profile will thank you with better jobs, too.
