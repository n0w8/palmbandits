By the end of GTA 5's story you had millions in the bank and barely anything to spend it on. GTA 6 flips that: money is tight, you have to go out and take it in the open world, and some parts of the story wait until you've saved enough. Here's how the system works according to Rockstar and the reviews, and the best ways to make money early on.

**As of November 18, 2026, 5 p.m. CET.** The figures come from reviews played before launch. We'll add our own experience and more precise numbers once the game is out.

## The short answer

- **Main source:** open-world robberies, not missions. PlayStation Universe says jobs usually pay little or nothing.
- **Start small:** stores and gas stations bring in a few thousand dollars, banks and gang hideouts a lot more.
- **Loot needs a fence:** jewelry, drugs, cars and marked bills only turn into money at the right fence.
- **Deposit your cash:** whatever you carry can be lost. The bank account is safe.

## Why money matters more in GTA 6

During the previews, Rockstar openly admitted that GTA 5 didn't give you enough to spend money on. In GTA 6, the story slows down or stops at certain points until you've reached a set amount. Rob Nelson of Rockstar North called making money off-mission an important part of the game.

The reviews back that up. GameSpot mentions several moments where you'll need to grind to progress. According to PlayStation Universe, one questline asks you to save $2 million. Vice describes money as a proper progression path you have to fight for.

## Cash, card, bank account

According to the previews, you hold money in three ways:

| Form | What to know |
|---|---|
| Cash | spend it right away, but you can lose it |
| ATM card | a separate form according to Rockstar, details still open |
| BuckMe bank account | safe, managed through a phone app |

On top of that comes loot in your duffel bag, which only counts once you've sold it. Citing the previews, Red Bull reports that cash is gone when you're "Wasted" or "Busted". So hit an ATM after every big score.

## The five robbery tiers

Rockstar splits open-world robberies into five tiers. The figures come from the reviews:

| Tier | What you take | What the reviews report |
|---|---|---|
| Stores and gas stations | register, back-room safe | usually a few thousand dollars, Shacknews counts 91 possible stores |
| Mini heists | cash vans and security trucks | no amounts given yet |
| Jewelry stores | jewelry for the fence | some stores let you disable the alarm first |
| Banks | cash, marked bills | around $140,000 for a planned bank job at Shacknews |
| Gang hideouts | cash, drugs, jewelry, marked bills, rare weapons or vehicles | Vice says some take more than one attempt |

According to Rockstar, small stores usually don't show up as map icons, while bigger targets appear on the map along with their potential take. You hear about robberies by exploring, through the story and from tips by other characters.

## Fences: without them, loot is worthless

Each type of loot has its own fence, for example for cars or jewelry. That has consequences:

- **Specialties:** According to PlayStation Universe, different fences pay more for certain goods.
- **Unlocks:** Previews say fences open up gradually through the story, so you may sit on valuable loot for a while before you can sell it.
- **Condition counts:** Damaged cars fetch less. Vice says vehicle fences can also tip you off to valuable cars in the police impound lot.
- **Cars without a fence:** Shacknews says Pay 'n' Spray buys stolen cars too, for a couple of thousand dollars.
- **Marked bills:** At Shacknews, they were laundered through an associate's business.

## Every little bit helps

- **Trunks:** Shacknews says you can loot other cars' trunks for weapons, ammo, cash and drugs.
- **ATMs:** Shacknews counts 454 ATMs, and 168 of them can be hooked up to a tow truck.
- **Hunting:** Kotaku came away from one hunt $450 richer.
- **Safes:** Blowing them is fast but can destroy what's inside. Finding the combination takes longer and keeps the full value. In stores, PlayStation LifeStyle says you can sometimes get the code by threatening staff.

## Six tips for your first hours

1. **Scout first:** Check cameras, alarms and exits. Vice says you can cut alarms and wipe footage so the police have no description.
2. **Start small:** Stores and gas stations are quick and give you a first cushion.
3. **Don't overfill the bag:** PlayStation Universe says loot is capped by bag size. Your partner or the trunk takes the rest, but a burning car destroys everything inside.
4. **Work clean:** Robbing efficiently without needless violence builds your Criminal Profile, and Vice says that unlocks new crimes.
5. **Rob as a pair:** Previews say pulling jobs together strengthens Jason and Lucia's relationship.
6. **Deposit right away:** Head to an ATM after every job, before the next chase goes wrong.

## Are there real-money purchases?

In August, Rockstar North's Rob Nelson told Kinda Funny there's no monetization in the game. GamingTrend's review, however, mentions a store with optional time savers, citing an extra 10 percent on fenced goods as an example. No other review we read mentions anything like that. We'll look for ourselves after launch and update this guide. GamingTrend adds that such a boost isn't needed anyway.

## Our take

Money is back as a real driver in GTA 6. That's the right call, but it brings some repetition: PlayStation Universe calls saving up for a story goal the only repetitive part. If you mix in small robberies early instead of only running missions, you won't hit a wall later. And keeping your profile clean gets you the better jobs.
