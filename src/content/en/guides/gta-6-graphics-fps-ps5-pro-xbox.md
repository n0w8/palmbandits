The question of which graphics mode to pick in GTA 6 has a short answer: there isn't one. The more interesting part is how differently the game runs across the four consoles. Digital Foundry published its analyses of PS5, PS5 Pro, Xbox Series X and Series S today. Here are the results, plus the settings on your console and TV that you can actually change.

**As of November 18, 2026, 5 p.m. CET.** All measurements come from pre-release review builds. We'll check after launch whether the day one build or later patches change anything.

## The short answer

- **No mode to choose:** GTA 6 runs at 30 FPS on every console. According to the reviews, there's no performance mode.
- **Best on PS5 Pro:** the steadiest 30 FPS, a near-4K image and the only console with ray-traced shadows.
- **PS5 and Series X:** around 1440p internally, mostly stable, with dips in heavy action.
- **Series S:** around 720p internally and often below 30 FPS. Digital Foundry recommends another console if you can.

## Is there a performance mode?

No. Rockstar confirmed 30 FPS for all consoles shortly before launch, PS5 Pro explicitly included. PlayStation LifeStyle's review also notes there's no performance mode.

Digital Foundry explains why: the bottleneck is the CPU, not the GPU. At 30 FPS the game has 33.3 milliseconds per frame to simulate its world. A 40 FPS mode would leave 25 milliseconds, which Richard Leadbetter says would take massive optimization or cutbacks. The PS5 Pro's CPU is only slightly faster than the base PS5's.

A datamine of the pre-load found a flag for a 120Hz mode but none for VRR. In theory that could point to a 40 FPS mode later on. None of it is confirmed. If Rockstar adds such a mode, we'll cover it here.

## All consoles compared

| Console | internal resolution | image | ray tracing | frame rate per Digital Foundry |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PS5 Pro | around 1440p | PSSR, passes for native 4K | lighting, reflections, shadows | near-flawless 30 FPS, brief 24 in a stress test |
| PS5 | around 1440p | basic upscaling, visibly 1440p | lighting, reflections | small dips, 23 FPS at worst |
| Xbox Series X | around 1440p, fairly fixed | softer than PS5 Pro | reflections, reports don't list more | mostly 30 FPS, around 20 in chaotic scenes |
| Xbox Series S | around 720p, 1080p output | softer, messier shadows | reflections kept | often mid to high 20s, around 20 in heavy scenes |

On Xbox Cloud Gaming, GTA 6 streams with Series S settings, according to Rockstar. Your connection affects the result on top of that.

## PS5 Pro: is it worth it for GTA 6?

For the cleanest image, yes. The Pro also renders at around 1440p internally, and PSSR turns that into something Digital Foundry says passes for native 4K. It also gets ray-traced shadows, exclusive to the Pro, which clean up the chunky shadow edges you see on the base PS5. Over more than 50 hours of normal play, Digital Foundry found no noticeable dips. Only an explosion stress test briefly pushed it down to 24 FPS.

The Pro isn't flawless either. GamingTrend mentions occasional PSSR artifacts, and Kotaku noticed slowdown on crowded Vice City streets and during big police shootouts, though it says it never became unplayable.

What the Pro won't give you is more frames per second. If you're upgrading just for GTA 6, you're buying a sharper and steadier picture, not a smoother one.

## PS5 and Xbox Series X: close behind

Both render at around 1440p internally and, according to Digital Foundry, look excellent, with praise for the lighting, reflections and hair. The differences show up under load:

- **PS5:** small but noticeable dips even in normal play. In the worst case, with a grenade launcher and exploding cars, it fell to 23 FPS and stayed there until the player left the area.
- **Series X:** mostly holds a clean 30 FPS, and cutscenes run without stutter. In wild firefights it reportedly drops a little lower than PS5, to around 20 FPS.

For most of your playtime, that barely matters. Digital Foundry still rates it as smoother than previous GTA games at launch.

## Xbox Series S: the problem child

The Series S keeps the key effects, including the strand-based hair and the ray-traced reflections, and Digital Foundry says the image looks better than its roughly 720p suggests. The issue is frame rate: even regular driving often sits in the mid to high 20s, and demanding fights drop to about 20 FPS. Digital Foundry's Oliver Mackenzie advises playing on another console for now if you have that option.

If a Series S is all you have, you can still jump in. The reviews describe cutscenes and quieter stretches as fine.

## The best settings for your console and TV

The game itself has no graphics modes. What you can tweak mostly lives on your console and TV:

1. **Turn on your TV's game mode:** It cuts input lag, which you'll notice when aiming at 30 FPS.
2. **Calibrate HDR:** Digital Foundry measured HDR peaks around 1,500 nits. On PS5, go to Settings > Screen and Video > Video Output > Adjust HDR. On Xbox, you'll find the calibration in the TV & display options.
3. **120Hz isn't a must:** At 30 FPS, a higher refresh rate brings no benefit right now.
4. **Leave motion blur on:** PSU says it makes 30 FPS feel smoother. Whether it can be switched off isn't confirmed yet.
5. **Tune your DualSense:** Rockstar says you can adjust haptic strength in the accessibility settings.

## Our take

30 FPS isn't news anymore, and the reviews show it's no drama for most players. The PS5 Pro is clearly the best choice, while PS5 and Series X deliver an excellent result with the occasional hitch. Only the Series S is a compromise you should know about going in. A performance mode would be a nice patch down the line, but don't count on it.
