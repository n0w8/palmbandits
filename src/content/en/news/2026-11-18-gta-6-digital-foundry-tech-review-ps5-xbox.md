The first big technical breakdown of GTA 6 is out, and the pecking order is clear: PS5 Pro is the best console version, Xbox Series S the weakest. Digital Foundry says it spent more than 50 hours with the game on PS5 Pro and compared every console. Rockstar has also named the resolutions itself, in an interview with Wccftech.

**As of: November 18, 2026, 4:50 pm CET (15:50 GMT).** We could not access Digital Foundry's article directly, so we report its findings as summarised by TweakTown, AltChar and Pure Xbox.

## Every console side by side

| Console | Internal resolution | Upscaling | Frame rate per Digital Foundry |
|---|---|---|---|
| PS5 | 1440p | Rockstar's own TAA, 4K output | mostly 30 FPS, frequent small dips, 23 FPS in the stress test |
| PS5 Pro | 1440p | PSSR 2, passes for native 4K | near-flawless 30 FPS, briefly 24 FPS in the stress test |
| Xbox Series X | 1440p | Rockstar's own TAA, 4K output | mostly 30 FPS, drops in chases and explosions |
| Xbox Series S | 720p | AMD FSR 4, 1080p output | often mid-to-high 20s, around 20 FPS when vehicles get wrecked |

Per AltChar, the resolutions and upscaling methods come from Rockstar, while the frame rates are Digital Foundry's measurements.

## PS5 Pro: the easy recommendation

In normal play the Pro held 30 FPS almost without exception, with even frame pacing and smooth cutscenes. Even in a deliberately brutal stress test, a grenade launcher and a street full of exploding cars, it only dipped to 24 FPS for a moment.

Two things separate it from the base PS5:

- **PSSR 2:** The image is also reconstructed from 1440p, but the analysts say it convincingly passes for native 4K. Rockstar has only now confirmed which PSSR version it uses.
- **Ray-traced shadows:** Exclusive to the Pro. They clean up the blocky shadow artefacts you see on the base PS5.

Both PS5 models use ray tracing for global illumination and reflections. Digital Foundry's verdict, paraphrased: the Pro is by far the best way to play.

## PS5 and Series X: good, with small hitches

The base PS5 shows frequent, small dips below 30 FPS in everyday play. Digital Foundry calls them very minor but noticeable. In the grenade launcher test it fell to 23 FPS and stayed there until the player left the area or died. The image is visibly softer than on the Pro.

Series X lands roughly where the PS5 does. The lighting, reflections and hair get praise, while missing shadows in places and an occasionally blurry image count against it.

## Xbox Series S: the version with the most cuts

Rockstar says Series S renders at 720p and upscales to 1080p, and it is the only console using AMD's FSR 4. The core look survives, but the frame rate is all over the place. Large stretches run in the mid-to-high 20s, and wrecking vehicles can drag it down to around 20 FPS. There are also plenty of visual cutbacks, including ray tracing and texture quality according to Pure Xbox. Digital Foundry calls it the hardest version to recommend.

## Why there is no 60 FPS mode

The analysts put the 30 FPS cap down to the CPU: simulating this much traffic, crowds and physics holds back even the Pro, whose processor is only marginally stronger than the base PS5's. Rockstar has not announced a 40 FPS mode for 120Hz TVs, although dataminer Tex2 found a 120Hz mode without VRR in the game's metadata.

## Our take

These numbers are better than many feared after the 30 FPS announcement. The 23 FPS on PS5 is a deliberately provoked worst case, not what you will see most of the time. If you own a Pro, you get a sharp, stable image with almost no hitching. Upgrading just for GTA 6 only makes sense to us if a softer image genuinely bothers you.

Honestly, Series S has a tough time here. If there is also a PS5 or Series X in your home, play it there. GTA 6 is perfectly playable on the small Xbox, it just will not always feel smooth.
