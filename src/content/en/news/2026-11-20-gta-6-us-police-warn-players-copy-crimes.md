GTA 6 is out, and several US police departments have used the moment for a public safety message: whatever you get up to in Leonida stays off real roads. IGN rounded up the posts overnight, and Dexerto, Fox 5 Atlanta and Newsweek have quoted them too.

## Who said what

- **Atlanta Police Department (Georgia):** In an X post on November 17, the department pointed out that GTA is made up while Atlanta is very much real. Drunk driving, reckless driving, car theft and violence have real consequences, it added, signing off with "Keep it in the game." A graphic from the department also lists street racing, gun violence and theft, Fox 5 Atlanta reports.
- **South Fulton Police Department (Georgia):** On Facebook, the department laid out its own ground rules. As quoted by WSB-TV, it reminded fans that games are fiction, that real crimes leave real victims, and that real life has no respawn and no undo option.
- **San Juan Police Department (Texas):** The department posted an Instagram video styled like the game, "Busted" screen included. An officer tells players that whatever happens in GTA VI should stay there, and that running red lights or stealing cars is strictly for the game, the Austin American-Statesman reports. IGN adds that the department stressed a real six-star wanted level ends in jail.
- **Floresville (Texas):** According to Dexerto, citing the San Antonio Express-News, local police made clear that the town belongs to neither Los Santos nor Vice City, its officers are not NPCs, and a fresh paint job will not clear a real wanted level.
- **Hays County Sheriff's Office (Texas):** Back in October, Newsweek reports, the office called I-35 "a highway, not a GTA lobby" on Facebook. A driver had clipped a patrol car during a late-night traffic stop and fled, and was caught shortly after.

## Not every department is worried

Greensboro police in North Carolina instead joked on X that GTA 6 could not arrive soon enough, since people would stay indoors and officers could finally rest. Newsweek says around a million people saw that post. IGN reads most of these posts as departments riding the release hype.

## What is confirmed and what is not

The posts themselves are confirmed and quoted consistently across several outlets. Whether the game affects real-world crime at all is an open question. Fox 5 Atlanta notes there is no evidence for a GTA 6 crime wave: research on earlier games points in different directions, and none of it shows that playing alone turns someone into a criminal.
