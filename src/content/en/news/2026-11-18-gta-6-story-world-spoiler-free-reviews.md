Reviewers could say a fair amount about GTA 6, but nowhere near everything. Put together, their reviews paint a clear picture: a story told across five chapters with multiple endings, a relationship between Jason and Lucia that you have to actively work on, and a version of Leonida where Vice City only gradually opens up to you. Here is what you can know before tonight without ruining anything.

**As of: November 18, 2026, 4:54 pm CET (15:54 GMT).** We stick to what the reviews themselves say. No late twists, no endings.

## How it begins

If you want to know nothing at all about the opening, skip ahead to the section on the world.

At the start, Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos are apart. According to GamePro, Lucia is in prison after a robbery, while Jason gets by in the Leonida Keys on petty hustles and smuggling jobs. After an early release, Lucia wears an ankle monitor and has to do community service, PC Games writes.

VGC describes what drives the pair: Jason tries to build a semi-legal life for them, and when that proves out of reach, they slide into serious crime. GamePro adds that along the way they come across two mysterious items that corrupt cops and other crooks are after. Beyond that, the reviews deliberately stay quiet about the plot.

## How the story is told

- **Five chapters:** That is GamePro's count, and it says most of the highlights are packed into chapter three.
- **A slow build:** PC Games compares the pacing to Red Dead Redemption 2. The story takes its time and plays out more like a rollercoaster than a classic rags-to-riches climb.
- **Linear main missions:** Story jobs are mostly linear, though less restrictive than in RDR2, according to GamePro.
- **Satire:** Alongside the action set pieces, PC Games mentions skits and satirical social media content in true series fashion.

## The relationship matters

The biggest change is the relationship itself. VGC reports a relationship meter that affects how the story plays out, including the endings. TheGamer says you have to put work into it, through dates, nights out at clubs and pulling jobs together. PC Games also confirms multiple endings without describing them.

## The world: Leonida grows with the story

VGC names the Leonida Keys as an early area. You can see Vice City on the horizon from the start, but you reach it as the story progresses. Beyond the city there is countryside, national parks, small towns, industrial zones and plenty of open ocean.

What makes it feel alive:

- **Random events and weather:** PC Games calls the open world the real star, with roadside events, a weather system and pedestrians who react to you.
- **A police force with a memory:** According to GamePro, cops recognise you by face, clothing, getaway car and whether you are alone or together. Security cameras play a part too, and you can have the footage wiped in certain places. From three stars, expect roadblocks.
- **Things to do:** PC Games lists minigames, race events and a proper race track. GamePro says some of the minigames could pass as standalone games.

## No difficulty setting, plenty of assists

There is no difficulty selection. Instead, GamePro says GTA 6 offers a wide range of accessibility options, including simplified minigames, easier combat with less damage taken and reduced vehicle damage.

## Our take

The reviews make one thing clear: GTA 6 is closer to Red Dead Redemption 2 than to GTA 5. If you expect non-stop action from minute one, prepare for a calmer start. The relationship meter is what interests us most. If it really shapes the endings, that is a genuine step for the series, and a good reason to treat Lucia and Jason as more than mission dispensers.

Our advice for the first few evenings: take your time in the Keys instead of rushing towards Vice City, and avoid any video with "ending" in the title until you have finished.
