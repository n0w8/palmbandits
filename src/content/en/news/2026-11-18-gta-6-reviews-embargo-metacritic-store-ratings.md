The first GTA 6 reviews are expected today, Wednesday, November 18, one day before release. Rockstar has never officially confirmed the date, but several reports agree on it. Until then, early user ratings on the PlayStation Store and a brief blip on Metacritic have fans talking.

**As of November 18, 2026, 6 AM CET (5 AM GMT).** At that point, no reviews from major outlets had been published.

## When does the review embargo lift?

- **Date:** Insider Gaming reported on November 17 that the embargo ends on November 18. Back in late October, the well-known insider NateTheHate had named the same day and said Rockstar was sending out review code to media.
- **Time:** According to Insider Gaming, the embargo is tied to marketing beats that go live when the game unlocks in New Zealand. That works out to 11 AM GMT, 12 PM CET or 6 AM Eastern. An earlier, equally unconfirmed forum post pointed to a window between 12:01 and 1:01 PM CET for the first Metacritic scores.
- **Staggered?** The same forum post claimed some sites and YouTube channels might not be allowed to publish videos until November 19. That's unconfirmed too.

In short: things should kick off around midday in Europe and early morning on the US East Coast. Nothing is guaranteed.

## Early stars on the PlayStation Store

Since November 10, when preloads started, ratings for GTA 6 have been showing up in the PlayStation Store backend. They aren't publicly visible; trackers like PS Store DB pull them through the API.

| Date | Number of ratings | Average | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| November 10 | 12 | 4.67 out of 5 | Dataminer, via Notebookcheck |
| November 17 | 183 | 4.88 out of 5 | PS Store DB, via Tech4Gamers |

Who is rating is unclear. Reports suspect media and creators who already have the game. Regular pre-order customers can only rate after launch. The Xbox Store is even more extreme: according to Notebookcheck, GTA 6 has sat at a perfect 5 out of 5 there after almost 10,000 ratings, and has done so for months, long before anyone could play it.

## Metacritic steps in

On November 17, user ratings suddenly appeared on Metacritic even though the game isn't out yet. According to iXBT Games, the user score stood at 6.5 out of 10 after just a handful of votes. The community quickly suspected trolls trying to drag the score down. Metacritic intervened, and shortly afterwards the user review section was empty again. How many votes there were is unknown.

## What the numbers actually tell you

Our take: very little. Store stars from an unknown group and a user score from people who can't have played the game say nothing about its quality. What matters are the critic reviews, which Metacritic and OpenCritic turn into an average once the embargo lifts.

The bar is high. GTA IV holds the series record on Metacritic with 98, while GTA V and Red Dead Redemption 2 both scored 97. Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick stressed back in May how much critical reception still matters to the company.

## What to watch out for today

1. **Avoid spoilers:** Video reviews often show story scenes. If you want to go in fresh, read text reviews with a verdict and skip the videos.
2. **Wait for the average:** The first five scores mean little. A reliable average only forms once enough reviews are in.
3. **Ignore user scores:** Nobody outside the press can actually play until release. Every user rating before November 19 is noise.
