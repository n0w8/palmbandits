Take-Two reported its fiscal second-quarter results on November 9, and they came in weaker than planned. Net bookings, the value of all content sold during the quarter, fell to $1.6 billion, below the company's own guidance. Even so, GTA's parent company reaffirmed its full-year target of $8 billion to $8.2 billion. The reason is GTA 6: for the current quarter, which includes the launch, Take-Two expects more than twice as much as in the previous one.

**As of November 10, 2026**

## The numbers at a glance

| Metric | Q2 (July to September 2026) | Same quarter last year |
|---|---|---|
| Net bookings | $1.60 billion | $1.96 billion |
| GAAP net revenue | $1.50 billion | $1.77 billion |
| Earnings per share | minus $0.29 | minus $0.73 |

The company's own forecast for the quarter had been $1.62 billion to $1.67 billion in net bookings. On the other hand, the net loss of $54 million was smaller than a year earlier. Take-Two reports ongoing player spending, such as in-game purchases in GTA Online or NBA 2K, as recurrent consumer spending. That fell 9 percent but still makes up 81 percent of net bookings.

## Why the quarter was weak

According to Game Developer, the main reason was NBA 2K27. The basketball game launched on September 4 and fell short of Take-Two's own expectations for the period. Many fans and investors were only looking at one date anyway: November 19.

## What Take-Two says about GTA 6

CEO Strauss Zelnick once again confirmed the November 19 release for PS5 and Xbox Series X|S in the earnings release. According to Zelnick, pre-orders are "continuing to reach unprecedented levels". He didn't give any concrete numbers. According to The Motley Fool, he also said, in essence, that the company wants to avoid declaring success before it happens and will report back after launch.

The forecasts in detail:

| Period | Net bookings according to Take-Two |
|---|---|
| Q3 (October to December 2026) | $3.35 billion to $3.5 billion |
| Fiscal year through March 2027 | $8.0 billion to $8.2 billion (reaffirmed) |

For comparison: the quarter that just ended brought in $1.6 billion. For the three months around the GTA 6 launch, Take-Two is therefore counting on roughly $1.75 billion to $1.9 billion more. Part of that will likely come from the regular holiday season; Take-Two doesn't break out how much is down to GTA 6.

## How the market reacted

Investors largely shrugged off the weak quarter. According to The Motley Fool, Take-Two stock rose as much as 10.6 percent in early US trading on November 10 and was still up around 7.5 percent at midday. The focus was clearly on the GTA 6 outlook.

## Our take

The numbers show how dependent Take-Two currently is on a single game. In its official list of upcoming titles, no other major project has a date, neither the next BioShock nor Judas nor Project ETHOS. The full-year forecast stands or falls with a smooth launch on November 19 and sales that meet sky-high expectations.

For players, the message is still clear: another delay is off the table. Ten days before release, Take-Two confirmed the date in black and white once more. The next earnings report, likely in February, will get interesting, since that's when Take-Two should share real GTA 6 sales figures for the first time.
